# Play-test backlog (from the user's play test, round 19)

Grouped into waves. Wave 1 is what made the game confusing; it goes first.

## Wave 1: make the loop clear (onboarding, controls, inventory)

### Onboarding and the first mission
- Start with nothing. First steps: open a supply bag, then craft your first thing.
- Dr. Calder physically leads the first run: she walks/runs ahead in the backrooms, stops at each step and shows it.
- A forced, one-thing-at-a-time tutorial: the screen darkens around what to do (spotlight), she explains, you do it.
  Teach every basic function, and the things a new player would not guess. Don't introduce too much at once.
- Her dialogue sounds human (casual, reactive, a little personal), not like a manual.
- A clear first mission with a time limit. No random quest first. Objectives always clear.
- A small quest tracker at the top of the screen.
- The watch starts with ONE app; more apps are installed later (each install taught when it happens).
- Teach: unlocking the scanner, opening it, scanning, using it to find a location.
- A radio quest: find the transistor to repair it.
- Teach crafting properly, and cooking.
- More clear 2D on-screen UI for the loop (tracker, prompts), in the game's style.

### Movement and controls
- Remove the roll (buggy). Add crouch, crawl and slide; vault/jump over low obstacles and slide under; get stuck far less.
- Running/leg movement looks stretched and weird: fix the unit's locomotion.
- Keybinds: B backpack, E pick up; odd or rarely used actions (whistle...) on rare/odd keys; no overlapping binds.
- No reaching through walls.
- Breaking walls only while holding a suitable tool (no free pop-ups for it).
- Right-click on things (corpses, containers, items) opens a context menu like Project Zomboid: plain text options,
  "more info" on right-click; pop-ups are plain text, no icons.
- No primary/secondary hand: the hotbar/inventory slots instead.
- Picked-up items go to the hotbar first.
- Planks and big items can only be carried (two hands), held/hugged with a proper animation, never floating;
  carried bodies hang/drag naturally instead of stiffly auto-rotating.

### Inventory, looting, first-person actions
- Hotbar and the first-person backpack scene restored and polished (user's, never redesigned), with a 2D on-screen readout.
- Very detailed looting: right-click a corpse → loot / search pockets / etc., first-person search cutscenes per container.
- Realistic detailed first-person QTEs (opening a box...), easier QTEs.
- Eating: detailed, e.g. open a bag of chips, pick chips out, eat, put the bag back.
- Cooking (system + first-person animation).
- Scanning much easier; tools and QTEs easier.

## Wave 2: crafting machines, body and health
- The press becomes simple (no printing interface). Printing moves to a separate machine you build yourself,
  fed by a filament maker you also build: industrial, every step detailed and realistic.
- Health check shows your 3D body model; opened by clicking the top-left body console.
- More realistic injuries (punching walls or metal hurts your hands, etc.).
- The press cutscene (another unit dragged and pressed): much heavier, realistic gore: organs, ribs, bones.

## Wave 3: art pass
- Remake every 3D item as super detailed pixel art with cool models; some interactive and playable, with physics
  (cloth, paper).
- All text and effects in pixel art (whistle rings, etc.).

## Wave 4: characters and the world
- Different playable characters; cloning and revive unlocked later.
- NPCs and residents far more realistic: talk more, smart player-like behaviour and activities, rarer.
- Creatures encountered more easily.
- Dr. Calder can walk in the backrooms (also needed in wave 1 for the tutorial).
